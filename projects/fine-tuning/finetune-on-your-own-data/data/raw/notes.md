# Studio notes (Maya)

## Closing up the studio

Turn off all wheels at the wall, wipe the splash pans, empty the slop buckets into reclaim, switch the extractor fan off last, and set the alarm with the code from the office. Check the kiln controller says either "firing" or "complete", never "error".

## Loading a bisque kiln

Bisque pieces can touch and stack. Put bowls rim to rim, heaviest pieces on the bottom shelf, and leave two fingers of space between pieces and the elements. Run the "Bisque 04 slow" program, which takes about 10 hours plus cooling.

## Loading a glaze kiln

Glazed pieces must not touch each other or the shelf. Check every foot is wiped clean of glaze, 5 mm up from the base. Use cookies under Rust Speckle and Midnight because they can run. Run the "Glaze 6 hold" program with a 10-minute hold at top temperature.

## Mixing Tidepool glaze

Tidepool is mixed in 5 kg batches. Weigh dry ingredients from the recipe card in the glaze room, add to 4.5 litres of water, sieve twice through an 80 mesh sieve, and adjust to a specific gravity of 1.45. Label the bucket with the date.

## When the kiln shows error E1

E1 means the kiln is heating slower than the program expects, usually because an element is worn out. Note the firing number, don't restart the program, and message Tom, who keeps the spare elements.

## Teaching the first wheel session

Spend the first 20 minutes on wedging and centring only. Most beginners push too hard; tell them to brace their elbow on their thigh and let the wheel do the work. Aim for one cylinder each by the end, even if it collapses.

## Trimming

Trim when the pot is leather hard: firm enough to hold its shape upside down, soft enough that a fingernail leaves a mark. Too dry and the tool chatters; too wet and the pot distorts.

## Ordering clay

Order Saltmarsh Buff from the supplier when we are down to 20 bags. A pallet is 40 bags and arrives in about a week. Store bags off the floor so they don't freeze in winter.

## Handling a kiln loss

If someone's piece breaks in the kiln, keep the pieces in a labelled bag, look for the cause (air bubble, thick base, glaze on the foot), and talk them through it in person. Offer a free taster slot if it was the studio's fault.

## Studio safety

No sweeping: dry clay dust is a silica hazard. Clean with a wet mop and sponges. Always wear a mask when mixing dry glaze materials. The first aid kit is above the sink and the fire blanket is next to the kilns.
